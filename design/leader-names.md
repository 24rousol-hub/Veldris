# Round 2: a nod to the Japanese original (author request, 2026-10-01)

The author asked for a nod to the game's Japanese origin and likes names that relate to the gym type. Eight agents each proposed Japanese-flavoured names for one gym, and a ninth reviewed every reading and meaning, dropped doubtful or clashing ones, and picked a recommendation. **Still PROPOSED.** Names are ASCII only, so macrons are dropped. **Not verified against Bulbapedia** (it could not be fetched): before you lock a name, search it there to make sure no Pokémon character uses it. The readings come from the agents' own knowledge, so a Japanese speaker should glance at the final eight.

| Gym | Recommended | Japanese source | Meaning | Other options (best first) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Bug | **HACHIMEL** | 蜂 hachi (bee), echoing 蜂蜜 hachimitsu (honey), plus Latin 'mel' (honey) | Bee and honey. An invented blend, parts all certain | HOTARU (蛍, firefly, a real girl's name), HACHI (蜂, bee), MARIBEE (Mari plus 'bee') |
| 3 Ghost | **SANZUFORD** | 三途 Sanzu, the river the dead cross in Japanese Buddhist belief, plus English 'ford' | 'The crossing', literally a mortician's job. Sounds like an English village surname | OBAKEWELL (お化け obake, ghost, hidden in 'Bakewell'), YOMI (黄泉, land of the dead), UTSUSEMI (空蝉, a cicada's shed shell) |
| 4 Steel | **HAGANE** | 鋼 hagane, steel (also the Japanese name of the Steel type) | Steel, said plainly: the deadpan joke | SABIFORD (錆 sabi, rust, plus 'ford'), TETSWORTH (鉄 tetsu, iron, by sound), KAJISMITH (鍛冶 kaji plus 'smith'), HONCHO (班長 hancho, squad leader, from which English got 'honcho') |
| 5 Ice | **WAKASAGI** | ワカサギ, the Japanese pond smelt | The fish caught by ice fishing through frozen lakes: an old fisherman with a thermos | HYOGA (氷河, glacier), YUKIWICK (雪 yuki, snow, plus 'wick'), KORIANDER (氷 kori, ice, plus 'coriander'), CORRIE (a glacier-carved hollow, sounds like kori) |

| 8 Fairy | **SUZURAN** | 鈴蘭, lily of the valley ('bell orchid') | A bell-shaped florist's flower that is highly poisonous: 'tougher than she looks'. | 9 Water | **MIZZLE** | 水 mizu (water), by sound. 'Mizzle' is a real English word for fine drizzle | A drizzle, for a man who is big, jolly and unhurried (subtle) | TODAI (灯台, lighthouse: the loudest nod), UMIWICK (海 umi, sea, plus 'wick'), ISARIBI (漁火, fishing lamps), SHIOMI (潮見, tide-watcher) |

**Dropped by the reviewer, with reasons.** MITSUBA (Honey's Japanese name), YUGAO (Calaba's Japanese name), TATARA (one vowel from TATORA, a Pokémon Champions character), MITZI (too close to MISTY), SAYONARA (stock stereotype), SUDOKU (the 'doku' is 独, 'single', not 毒, 'poison'), YOSEI (a label, not a name).

**The set at a glance.** 3 pure Japanese words (HAGANE, WAKASAGI, SUZURAN), 3 Japanese and English blends (HACHIMEL, SANZUFORD, ASEBY), 2 English names with a hidden pun (TOBIN, MIZZLE). Only SANZUFORD and ASEBY use a village ending, so they do not all feel alike. ASEBY and GATSBY both end in -BY (swap ASEBY for GEDOKU if that bothers you).

**Dials.** Louder nod: TOBIN to KAZAMI, MIZZLE to TODAI. Lighter nod: HAGANE to SABIFORD, but then change SANZUFORD to YOMI or OBAKEWELL so two names do not share '-ford'. You can also mix rounds: for example BRIONY (round 1) with HAGANE (round 2).
